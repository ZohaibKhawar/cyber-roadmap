Study plans

Week-by-week plans for the certifications pen testers take most, from Security+ to OSCP+. Each one is built on the exam's official objectives and on labs made for practice, and ends with the habits that help on exam day.

Security+ PenTest+ eJPT PNPT CPTS OSCP+ Junior to senior

Why there are no exam dumps here

  • “Dumps” are real exam questions copied out by earlier candidates. Every exam on this page makes you agree not to share or use its questions, and CompTIA, OffSec, INE, TCM and Hack The Box can cancel results and revoke certifications over it.
  • They don't work for hands-on exams anyway. eJPT, PNPT, CPTS and OSCP+ put you in a lab and grade what you do and what you write.
  • Interviewers test skills, not answers. Someone who memorised a dump is easy to spot in the first technical question.
  • What we offer instead: the practice quiz, with over a hundred original questions in exam style, each with the reasoning behind the answer.

How to study for any of them

  1. Start from the official objectives. Download them, turn each line into a checklist item, and tick it off only when you could teach it to someone else.
  2. Learn, then lab, then write. Watch or read about a topic, practise it on a legal lab the same week, and write short notes in your own words.
  3. Test yourself early. Recalling an answer from memory teaches more than rereading. Use the practice quiz before you feel ready.
  4. Rehearse the real thing. For multiple-choice exams, sit timed practice tests. For hands-on exams, run a full mock: a set of practice machines, a fixed clock and a finished report.

Hours and weeks below are our rough estimates for someone with IT basics studying about eight hours a week. Move faster or slower as you need; the order matters more than the pace.

Foundations · multiple choice

CompTIA Security+

8 weeksabout 60–80 hours

Questions
Up to 90, multiple choice and performance-based
Time
90 minutes
Pass mark
750 on a 100–900 scale
Versions
V8 (SY0-801) launches about 17 Nov 2026; V7 (SY0-701) retires in English on 11 Jun 2027

What it covers (V7): general security concepts; threats, vulnerabilities and mitigations; security architecture; security operations, the largest domain; and security program management and oversight. Check which version your course teaches.

  1. Week 1 Download the objectives and take the fundamentals quiz cold. Learn the basics: the CIA triad, control types, cryptography, certificates and authentication.
  2. Weeks 2–3 Threats, vulnerabilities and mitigations: malware, social engineering, application and network weaknesses, and the controls that reduce each.
  3. Week 4 Security architecture: network design, cloud and virtualisation, resilience, backups and recovery.
  4. Weeks 5–6 Security operations: identity and access, monitoring and logging, vulnerability management and incident response.
  5. Week 7 Program management: governance, risk, compliance, third-party risk, audits and security awareness.
  6. Week 8 Timed practice tests. Review every wrong answer until you know why each other option was wrong too. Practise performance-based questions.

Pen testing · multiple choice

CompTIA PenTest+

10 weeksabout 80–100 hours

Questions
Up to 90, multiple choice and performance-based
Time
165 minutes
Pass mark
750 on a 100–900 scale
Exam
PT0-003

What it covers: engagement management; reconnaissance and enumeration; vulnerability discovery and analysis; attacks and exploits; post-exploitation and lateral movement. Planning, scoping and reporting carry real weight, so don't treat them as an afterthought.

  1. Weeks 1–2 Engagement management: scope, rules of engagement, law, methodologies, and how findings are reported and communicated.
  2. Weeks 3–4 Reconnaissance and enumeration in a legal lab: public information, then discovering hosts and services and recording what you find.
  3. Week 5 Vulnerability discovery and analysis: scanners, reading their output, and confirming findings by hand.
  4. Weeks 6–8 The attack topics in the objectives, practised only on platforms built for it, such as TryHackMe's junior pen tester path and PortSwigger's free web labs.
  5. Week 9 Post-exploitation and lateral movement concepts, and the cleanup and reporting expected at the end of a test.
  6. Week 10 Timed practice tests, then the weakest domain again.

What it covers (INE's weights): host and network penetration testing 35%; assessment methodologies 25%; host and network auditing 25%; web application penetration testing 15%.

  1. Weeks 1–2 Refresh networking and Linux, and learn the phases of a test so you always know what you're doing and why.
  2. Weeks 3–4 Information gathering and enumeration in labs, with one note per host.
  3. Weeks 5–6 Host and network testing labs on INE's own course or TryHackMe's junior pen tester path.
  4. Week 7 Web basics: HTTP, a proxy, and PortSwigger's apprentice-level labs.
  5. Week 8 Three or four easy retired lab machines from start to finish, with notes good enough to answer questions from.

Feels like
A small real engagement: public research, the external side, then the internal network

What it covers: open-source research, external testing, internal network and Active Directory testing, and presenting your results to a client.

  1. Weeks 1–4 TCM's core ethical hacking course, with every lab done in your own environment, and a small home Windows domain built along the way.
  2. Weeks 5–6 Open-source research and external testing courses. Practise turning what you find into scope-aware notes.
  3. Weeks 7–9 Windows and Linux privilege escalation courses, and Active Directory practice on your lab and on practice platforms.
  4. Week 10 Write a full report on a lab you finished, using a template, and time how long it takes.
  5. Weeks 11–12 A mock engagement: several practice machines over a long weekend, then the report, then explain it out loud in 15 minutes.

What it covers: the whole of an internal and external engagement, with a heavy focus on Active Directory, web applications, and the report.

  1. Months 1–2 The path's first third: methodology, networking, information gathering and the core tools. Take notes per module in your own words.

Three standalone machines (60 points) and an Active Directory set of three, starting from given credentials (40 points)

What it covers: the PEN-200 course: methodology, enumeration, web and network attacks, privilege escalation on Linux and Windows, Active Directory, and professional reporting. Points only count if the report proves them.

  1. Month 1 Work through PEN-200 in order, doing the exercises rather than just reading.
  2. Months 2–3 The course's challenge labs. Keep a personal checklist of what to look for at each stage and improve it after every machine.
  3. Month 4 Extra practice on OffSec Proving Grounds and retired Hack The Box machines, including Active Directory sets.
  4. Months 5–6 At least one full mock: a set of unseen practice machines, a 24-hour clock, sleep and breaks planned, and a finished report the next day.

From first tester job to senior

The senior step on the roadmap →

Certificates get you the first job. After that, what makes you senior is experience on real engagements and the judgement that comes with it.

  1. Year 0–1 in the jobJunior tester

    Learn your firm's methodology and report style, shadow leads, write findings that pass review, and finish a hands-on cert if you haven't.

  2. Years 1–3Tester

    Run tests on your own, pick a specialty (web, cloud, Active Directory, mobile), and build templates or tools your team uses.

  3. Years 3–5Senior tester

    Lead engagements, scope work, review reports, present to executives, and take an advanced cert such as OSEP, OSWE, CRTO or BSCP.

  4. Years 5+Lead or principal

    Run a team, own client relationships, lead red team or threat-led work, and publish research or speak at meetups.

Typical ranges, not rules. Practise the judgement side with the senior scenarios in the practice quiz.

Only practise on systems you own or have written permission to test. Unauthorised access is a crime in Canada, the US and most other countries.
