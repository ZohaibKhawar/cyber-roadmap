Cyber security jobs and pay

What each job is like day to day, how people actually get into it, and what official data says about pay, growth and how easy it is to get hired, in the US and in Canada.

What this means for you

  • Security work is growing far faster than most jobs, and unemployment in tech is lower than the national rate.
  • But growth is concentrated in experienced roles. Ontario's outlook is "limited" for 2025–2027, and many teams cut budgets in 2024–25. Expect a competitive search for your first security job.
  • That's why both roadmaps include IT jobs as stepping stones. BLS lists "less than 5 years" of related work experience and a bachelor's degree as typical for information security analysts, but plenty of people get in with a college diploma, certs and IT experience.

How people usually get in

  1. Year 0–2IT support or developer

    Help desk, desktop support, junior developer. Learn how real companies run.

  2. Year 1–3Admin or SOC analyst

    System or network admin, or tier 1 SOC. Your first security title often comes here.

  3. Year 3–6Specialist

    Pen tester, incident responder, cloud or application security engineer, GRC analyst.

  4. Year 6+Senior or lead

    Senior engineer, architect, red team lead, security manager.

Typical ranges, not rules. Some people move faster with strong lab work and a good network; some stay happily in one stage for years.

Entry security role

SOC analyst

Watching alerts in a security operations centre, deciding which are real, investigating and escalating. Often shift work.

How people get in
Security+ or CySA+, hands-on blue team labs, and ideally a year in IT.
Skills used daily
SIEM searches (Splunk, Sentinel), EDR, email and log analysis, clear notes.
Pay and outlook
$129,180 · +21% by 2035BLS, information security analysts (all levels). Entry SOC pay sits toward the bottom 10%, under $75,090.

Entry–mid

GRC / risk and compliance analyst

Writing policies, running risk assessments, preparing for audits such as SOC 2 and ISO 27001, and tracking fixes.

How people get in
From IT, audit, business or law backgrounds. Strong writing matters more than coding.
Skills used daily
Frameworks (NIST CSF, ISO 27001), spreadsheets, interviews, report writing.
Pay and outlook
No separate official dataUsually counted with information security analysts.

Incident responder / forensics

Leading the response when there's a real breach: containing it, finding out what happened and how far it spread.

How people get in
Usually after one to three years in a SOC.
Skills used daily
Windows forensics, timelines, EDR, memory analysis, calm communication.
Pay and outlook
No separate official dataCounted with information security analysts. Often includes on-call pay.

Mid

Penetration tester / security consultant

Testing networks and applications with permission to find weaknesses, then writing the report that gets them fixed.

How people get in
IT, SOC or developer experience, a portfolio of lab write-ups, and a hands-on cert such as PNPT, CPTS or OSCP+.
Skills used daily
Web and network testing, Active Directory, scripting, report writing, client calls.
Pay and outlook

Senior

Red team operator

Running long, goal-based exercises that test whether a company's defenders can detect and respond, then helping them improve.

How people get in
Several years as a pen tester first.
Skills used daily
Planning around realistic threats, Active Directory, working closely with the blue team.
Pay and outlook

Security architect

Senior

Security manager / CISO

Leading the security team, setting priorities and budgets, and reporting risk to executives and the board.

How people get in
Usually 5+ years in IT or security, plus leadership experience.
Skills used daily
Risk, budgets, hiring, communication, frameworks.
Pay and outlook
$175,140 · +16% by 2035BLS, computer and information systems managers (includes non-security IT managers).
Side income

Bug bounty hunter

Finding and reporting security bugs to companies that run public bounty programs, and getting paid per valid report.

How people get in
Strong web testing skills and lots of patience. Read each program's scope and rules first.
Skills used daily
Web and API testing, recon, clear reports.
Pay and outlook
IrregularMost people earn little or nothing at first. Better treated as practice and portfolio than a salary.
